Conventions (explicit)

  • Constellations normalised to Es = 1 (mean symbol energy). Bits/symbol k = log₂M, so Eb = 1/k.
  • N0 = Eb / (Eb/N0) = 1 / (k·γ), γ = 10^(dB/10).
  • Complex noise n = nI + j·nQ with total variance E|n|² = N0, i.e. σ² = N0/2 per real dimension.
  • BFSK: coherent orthogonal, symbols e₀=(1,0), e₁=(0,1) (Es=Eb=1), ML detection = larger correlator output. All others: Gray-mapped, minimum-distance detection.
  • Zero-error points are plotted as upper bounds (rule of three, 95%: BER < 3/N, hollow ▽), never as BER = 0.

Expected physical effect (written before running)
  • BPSK and QPSK (Gray) lie on the same BER-vs-Eb/N0 curve, Q(√(2Eb/N0)): QPSK is two independent BPSK streams, each getting the full Eb.
  • Coherent BFSK needs ≈3 dB more Eb/N0 than BPSK for the same BER (orthogonal vs antipodal signalling, Q(√(Eb/N0))).
  • Higher order (8-PSK, 16-QAM, 64-QAM) need progressively more Eb/N0 since points are closer together at fixed Es; expect ≈4 dB, ≈6 dB... shifts relative to QPSK rising with M.
  • Theory for 8-PSK and M-QAM is a nearest-neighbour approximation: accurate at high SNR, slightly off (optimistic/pessimistic) at low SNR.
  • At very low SNR all BERs tend to 0.5; at very high SNR errors vanish and only an upper bound can be reported.
